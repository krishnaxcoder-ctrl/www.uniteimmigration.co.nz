Partner of a New Zealander Resident Visa

This visa allows you to live in New Zealand permanently if you are in a genuine and stable relationship with a New Zealand citizen or resident, and you have been living together for at least 12 months.

Partner eligibility

To support your residence application, your partner must meet Immigration New Zealand’s eligibility rules. This includes:

They can only have supported one previous partner for residence.

In the past 5 years, they must not have supported or been included in another partner residence application.

They must not be liable for deportation or have any deportation liability suspended.

Relationship requirements

You must show that your relationship is genuine and ongoing. This typically includes evidence that you:

Live together as a couple

Share responsibilities and, where relevant, finances

Spend time together and are recognised as a couple

Have a clear and consistent relationship timeline

Dependent children

You may include dependent children aged 24 or younger, if they meet the dependency requirements and you can provide:

Proof of identity and relationship (birth certificates/adoption papers)

Health and character documents (as required)

Custody evidence for children aged 15 or younger, where applicable

Special situations

In some cases, if your partner is a New Zealand citizen and you have been living together overseas for 5 years or more, Immigration New Zealand may consider granting a Permanent Resident Visa (depending on eligibility and travel history).

FAQs

Does time spent living apart count toward the 12-month living together requirement?

Generally no. Immigration New Zealand places strong weight on physical cohabitation. Short periods apart may be acceptable if well explained and supported by evidence, but long separations usually weaken an application.

What if my partner has supported a previous partner’s residence application?

A New Zealand partner can usually support only one previous partner for residence. If this limit is exceeded, the application may be declined.

Can my partner’s past convictions affect my application?

Yes. Your partner must meet character requirements. Convictions for domestic violence or sexual offences are treated seriously and can prevent support of a partnership residence application.

What type of evidence causes the most delays or refusals?

Applications are commonly delayed where evidence is one-sided, inconsistent, or limited to photos and messages. Immigration New Zealand expects strong joint evidence, such as shared finances, shared address history, and third-party recognition of the relationship.

What happens if Immigration New Zealand is not satisfied with our relationship evidence?

Immigration New Zealand may request further information, conduct interviews, or issue a Potentially Prejudicial Information (PPI) letter. If concerns are not addressed adequately, the application maybe declined.

What if I’m not sure if this visa applies?
